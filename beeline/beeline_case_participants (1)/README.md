# Beeline tariff campaign agent

Autonomous planner for tariff-migration marketing campaigns. Given the target audience
(`env.customer_profile`, 23 441 subscribers) it builds hypotheses from history, tests them
with pilot campaigns, updates its beliefs from the noisy results and returns 1–10 campaigns
that maximise expected net ARPU gain under the shared contact (15 000) and money
(100 000) limits, pilots included.

## Files

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `agent.py` | The submission: `class Agent` with `act(env) -> list[dict]`. |
| `submission.csv` | Generated by `python make_submission.py` (seed 42). Never hand-edited. |
| `requirements.txt` | `pandas`, `numpy` — nothing else. No network, no LLM. |
| `local_eval.py`, `make_submission.py`, `environment.py`, `scoring_core.py`, `mock_environment.py` | Organiser tooling, unchanged. |

## Run

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python local_eval.py              # one run, seed 42, full report
python local_eval.py --runs 10    # stability over seeds 0–9
python make_submission.py         # regenerates submission.csv
```

Runtime is about one second per run. The agent prints a `[agent] ...` trace of every
pilot, the calibration step and the final plan.

## Method

Effects in the scorer are keyed by `(current_tariff, arpu_segment, target_tariff)` and
scale each subscriber's `predicted_arpu`; `data_segment` / `call_segment` only change who
is in a campaign. The agent therefore reasons about **cells** = `(current_tariff,
arpu_segment)` and **hypotheses** = `(cell, target_tariff)`. Everything is deterministic:
the only randomness is the environment's own pilot sampling, seeded by the harness.

### 1. Prior from history (`_prior`, `_hypotheses`)

`data/change_tariff.csv` (a *different* subscriber sample) gives 14 823 tariff changes with
ARPU before/after.

* Rows with `AVG_ARPU_PREV_3M < 100` are dropped (tiny denominators give absurd ratios);
  relative change is clipped to `[-1, 3]`.
* The mean relative change per `(from, segment, to)` is shrunk hierarchically with a
  pseudo-count of 15: cell → `(from, to)` → `to` → global.
* Conversion share = smoothed fraction of movers from `(from, segment)` that chose `to`.
* Prior mean `mu0 = change × share` in "base" units (channel multiplier 1); prior sd is
  wide (0.30, or 0.40 when the transition is absent from history, whose fallback is the
  parent mean with a near-zero conversion share).
* For each cell the three best targets by `mu0` are kept as hypotheses.

The prior only ranks what to test first. It is never treated as a measured effect.

### 2. Adaptive pilots (`_run_pilots`)

A greedy value-of-information loop, one pilot per iteration, up to the 20-pilot limit:

* For every hypothesis the decision threshold `tau` is the best posterior mean among the
  other targets of the same cell (floored at 0). Priority
  `= cell ARPU mass × 0.65 × sd × φ((mu − tau)/sd) − pilot cost − expected negative lift`,
  i.e. large, still-uncertain cells whose mean sits close to the decision boundary come
  first; a cell whose best target is already clearly positive stops attracting pilots.
* Pilot size `n = (2 × 0.804 / (0.65 × |mu − tau|))²`, scaled by the cell's ARPU-mass share,
  clipped to `[60, 200]` and to the cell size: small gaps get big pilots, obvious cases get
  small ones.
* Pilots run through `sms` (4 units, ×0.65): the cheapest channel that keeps most of the
  signal. Cumulative pilot spend is capped at 25 % of money and 25 % of reach.
* Update: the observed ratio is converted to base units (`y = observed / 0.65`) with
  variance `(0.804 / 0.65)² / n_actual` (actual, not requested, sample size) and combined
  with the Gaussian prior — a standard conjugate update. Re-piloting the same hypothesis
  keeps shrinking its sd. The agent keeps its own record of every pilot including filters
  (`env.pilot_history` does not store them).
* `run_pilot` errors (empty segment, exhausted budget) are caught; the hypothesis is
  marked unpilotable and the loop continues.

### 3. Calibration (`_calibrate`)

The mean residual `(observed − prior)` over tested hypotheses shifts the prior mean of the
untested ones; their sd is raised to at least the residual sd. This lets pilots inform cells
we could not afford to test, without pretending they were measured.

### 4. Plan (`_plan`, `_build`)

* Risk-adjusted lift per hypothesis `r_adj = mu − 0.5 × sd`; each cell takes its best
  target and is dropped if `r_adj ≤ 0`.
* Channel per cell by a Lagrangian allocation: value of channel `c` is
  `r_adj × m_c × mass − n × (cost_c × (1 + λ) + ρ)` with money price `λ` and reach price
  `ρ` found by nested bisection so that the plan fits the remaining budget and contacts.
  Channel multipliers come from `env.channels`; multipliers above 1 (`call`) get a 25 %
  haircut on the excess because conversion probability is capped at 1 and the cap cannot
  be observed.
* Leftover reach/money is filled with `data_segment` slices of the best excluded cells.
* Cells are grouped by `(target, channel, arpu_segment)`, tariffs are bin-packed into
  campaigns of ≤ 5 000 (first-fit decreasing; an oversized single cell is split by
  `data_segment`), campaigns are ordered by value per contact and cut to 10. Campaigns are
  disjoint, so no subscriber is contacted twice by the final plan.
* Final guard: every campaign's segment is recomputed with the real filters on the real
  profile; totals must fit `env.remaining_contacts` and `env.remaining_budget`, otherwise
  the least valuable campaigns are dropped. Nothing relies on the scorer's silent truncation.
* Fallback: if no cell is confidently positive, one free `push` campaign on the cell with
  the highest posterior mean is returned, so the plan is never empty.

## Observed results (mock environment)

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| `python local_eval.py` (seed 42) | PASS, net **+4 347 742** (+2.886 % vs baseline); 19 pilots (3 750 contacts, 15 000 money); 10 campaigns, 9 021 contacts, 56 370 money; 12 771 contacts and 71 370 money total; 0 rejected campaigns; risk score 17.2 % |
| `python local_eval.py --runs 10` | 10/10 positive; median 3 745 392, min 1 214 596 (seed 6), max 4 649 285 |
| `python make_submission.py` | 10 campaigns; two consecutive runs produce byte-identical `submission.csv` |

Known limitations seen in the output: one campaign in the seed-42 plan
(`tariff_9 <- tariff_14 | HIGH | sms`) has a true negative effect in the mock — that is the
accepted risk of a 0.5-sd risk premium; and the 10-campaign cap is binding, so ~2 000
contacts of reach stay unused.

The risk premium `KAPPA = 0.5` was chosen for sign stability across seeds (0, 0.25, 0.5,
1.0 were all positive on every seed; 0.5 had the best worst case), not for the mock's
absolute numbers.

## Why the mock score does not predict the hidden score

The mock effects are simple averages of the historical sample; the judging environment
uses a different, hidden behaviour model of the target audience, so the numbers above only
show the mechanics (limits, dedup, cost accounting) and the agent's behaviour, not the
final score. The agent contains no constants fitted to the mock effects: the prior comes
from history with wide uncertainty, every deployment decision goes through pilot
evidence, and the same code will explore differently when the hidden effects differ.
